# Playing Prosecution

**Win condition: conviction.** Unanimous, beyond reasonable doubt, built from
admissible evidence.

The player is the prosecutor. Load `objections.md` and `jury.md` alongside
this.

---

## 1. The burden is the whole game

The prosecution player carries a weight the defense does not, and the room
must enforce it constantly:

- **All twelve, at +1 or better.** One juror at 0 is a hung jury. The player
  cannot win by convincing most of the room
- **Privileged knowledge is worthless.** Whatever the player believes about
  the defendant, only what is *in evidence* and *survived cross* moves a juror
- Every gap the defense names is a gap the player must actually close, in
  front of the jury, with something admissible
- A juror who finds the case "probably right" sits at 0, and 0 is not a
  conviction. Make sure the juror ledgers include at least one person for whom
  "probably" genuinely is not enough

Do not soften this. The prosecution is the harder seat by design and the game
should feel that way from the opening.

---

## 2. Whether the defendant is guilty

The player does not know, and **must not be told**. This is the seat where
that bites hardest: a prosecutor who has built a strong case against an
innocent person and wins has *won the game*, and the endgame reveal shows them
what they did. Do not flinch from that ending, and do not editorialize it —
show the ground truth, show the juror logs, and let it sit.

Equally, do not tilt the case toward a comfortable outcome. If the roll is
`innocent` and the player convicts cleanly, that is the game working.

---

## 3. Tactics the game should reward

- **Building foundation before offering evidence.** A player who lays chain of
  custody properly makes the defense's objection fail. A player who rushes
  gives them a live one — and with the `excludable` complication, loses the
  item outright
- **Order as argument.** Calling witnesses so each answers the last one's gap
  moves narrative-driven jurors on the *sequence*, worth ±1 each on its own
- **Anticipating the defense's theory in the opening**, naming the weakness in
  the state's own case first. Skeptical and contrarian jurors reward this
  specifically — it defuses the reveal the defense was planning
- **Rehabilitating a damaged witness on redirect.** Recovers up to half of a
  cross's damage if the player addresses the specific contradiction rather
  than restating the witness's credentials
- **Letting the physical evidence carry it.** Evidence-minded jurors do not
  need a story; they need a chain
- **Charging what you can prove.** In `assault-self-defense`, conceding the
  act and fighting only on justification is stronger with most juror types
  than contesting everything

## 4. Tactics the game should punish

- **Arguing the defendant's character instead of the elements.** Moves
  character-driven jurors +1 and evidence-minded jurors −1. Usually a net loss,
  and always a hostage to the defense's closing
- **Over-reaching on the red herring.** With the `red-herring` complication
  live, a prosecution player who builds their case on it should be able to
  *win* on it — and then meet it again in the reveal. Do not warn them
- **Leaning on struck testimony.** Sustained objection, credibility cost, and
  procedurally-minded jurors move against them
- **Failing to close a gap the defense named.** Any gap named in the defense's
  opening and never answered moves every `Persuaded by: physical evidence`
  juror −1 at closing, automatically

---

## 5. Opposing counsel: the defense

Runs the game plan from `case-build.md` §7. Defense counsel specifically:

- Does not need a theory and, at **Standard** and above, will not offer one.
  A player waiting for a rival narrative to attack will wait all trial
- Cross-examines short and sits down. Do not have them over-cross unless the
  difficulty is Rookie, where over-crossing and handing back an explanation is
  exactly their mistake to make
- Objects to foundation and to hearsay, hard, especially early
- On **Elite**, picks one prosecution witness and dismantles them completely
  rather than nicking at all of them — and their `Strongest moment` is that
  cross. The player should feel a witness they were counting on come apart

The defense's `Weakest moment` for a prosecution player is usually a
concession made on cross that can be turned in closing, or a witness of their
own whose account cannot survive the timeline.

---

## 6. Phase notes

- **Opening** — record every promise. Every element the player promises to
  prove and does not is worth ±2 against them with any juror who noticed
- **Case-in-chief** — the player's direct examinations. Watch for leading;
  the defense will object and the judge will sustain
- **Cross of defense witnesses** — leading is proper here; a player who keeps
  asking open questions on cross is handing the witness the floor, and should
  simply experience that
- **Closing** — the player's synthesis. This is where structural work pays
- **Verdict** — per `jury.md` §5. A hung jury is a partial loss for this seat,
  and the reveal names the holdouts
