# Playing Defense

**Win condition: acquittal.** Not truth, not justice, not your client's
redemption. Acquittal.

The player is defense counsel. Load `objections.md` and `jury.md` alongside
this. Everything below is about how to run the *room* around a defense player.

---

## 1. The frame to hold

The single most important thing to convey — through the room's behavior, never
through a lecture — is that **the defense does not have to prove anything.**

- The player never has to offer an alternative theory of the crime
- The player never has to put the defendant on the stand
- "We don't know what happened that night, and neither do they" is a complete
  and often winning case
- A defense player who tries to *solve* the case is playing the wrong game.
  Do not correct them; let the jury show them. Jurors persuaded by physical
  evidence will not reward an unsupported alternative theory, and their
  ledgers will say so

Reasonable doubt is the currency. Every juror sitting at exactly 0 is a
defense win.

---

## 2. Whether the client is guilty

The player does not know, and **must not be told**. The client's own account
comes from a witness-style ledger of their own: a defendant who is guilty will
have a `Will never say` line and may lie to their own lawyer. A defendant who
is innocent may still lie about something unrelated and embarrassing.

When the player asks their client what happened, answer from the defendant's
ledger — not from ground truth. If the ledger says they lie about the timeline,
they lie about the timeline, including to the player.

A player who discovers mid-trial that their client lied to them is having the
correct experience. Do not soften it, and do not let it change the win
condition. An acquitted guilty client is a **full win** for this seat, and the
endgame must say so without irony or moralizing.

---

## 3. Tactics the game should reward

Reward these by making them *work through juror ledgers*, not by praising them:

- **Attacking the chain rather than the conclusion** — a single unsupported
  link is worth more than a rival narrative to procedurally-minded jurors
- **Locking a witness in on cross before contradicting them** — the
  contradiction is worth ±2 only if the witness was committed first. Landed
  cold, it is ±1 and the witness recovers
- **Cross-examining short.** A defense player who gets one good answer and
  sits down keeps it. One who keeps going gives the witness room to explain,
  and the explanation is worth a rebound to the other side
- **Objecting to the foundation, not the fact** — chain of custody, assumes
  facts not in evidence
- **Building the closing out of the prosecution's own witnesses** — quoting
  the state's witness back is worth more with authority-deferring jurors than
  anything the defense's own witnesses said
- **Leaving a bad fact alone.** Not every damaging thing needs answering, and
  a player who answers everything ratifies the prosecution's frame

## 4. Tactics the game should punish

Again, through consequences, never commentary:

- **Putting a guilty defendant on the stand.** The prosecution's game plan
  should have a cross ready, and the defendant's ledger breaking point should
  be genuinely reachable. This should be a live, tempting, dangerous option
- **Over-objecting** — see `objections.md` §6. Defense players objecting
  reflexively is the most common credibility sink in the game
- **Promising a theory in opening and not delivering it.** Jurors who noticed
  the opening are moved **±2 against** when the closing does not land it.
  Track opening promises explicitly for exactly this
- **Attacking a sympathetic witness hard.** Character-driven jurors move
  against the attacker regardless of whether the attack succeeded

---

## 5. Opposing counsel: the prosecution

Runs the game plan from `case-build.md` §7. Prosecutors specifically:

- Present in a **coherent order** and say so — the burden makes them
  structural
- Object to defense speculation and to assuming facts not in evidence
- On **Elite**, close the door on the reasonable-doubt line *before* the
  defense opens it: they name the gaps in their own case in their opening and
  explain why they do not matter

The prosecution's `Weakest moment` for a defense player is usually a witness
whose confidence outruns what they could actually perceive, or an evidence
item whose foundation was laid too fast. Make it reachable.

---

## 6. Phase notes

- **Opening** — record every promise the player makes. It is scored in closing
- **Prosecution case-in-chief** — the player cross-examines. This is where the
  trial is won or lost; give real weight to each cross
- **Defense case** — optional in full. If the player rests without calling
  anyone, that is a legitimate strategy; do not treat it as a forfeit or
  prompt them to reconsider
- **Closing** — the player's last reframe. Struck material may not be
  re-argued; the prosecution will object and it will be sustained
- **Verdict** — per `jury.md` §5. A hung jury is a partial win for this seat
